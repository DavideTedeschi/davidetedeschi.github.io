The [Nonlinear Ultrafast Spectroscopy Laboratory](/lab/) can perform time-resolved, nonlinear and Raman measurements on thin films, 2D materials and heterostructures, devices, molecular solutions and biological samples. This page lists the installed instrumentation and the capabilities that are still being developed.

## Ultrafast source

* A Light Conversion PHAROS 20 W femtosecond laser coupled to an ORPHEUS optical parametric amplifier, delivering pulses tunable from 300 nm to 3 µm, with durations from 50 to 200 fs, at a repetition rate of about 100 kHz

## Pump–probe and transient absorption

* Femtosecond pump–probe and transient absorption spectroscopy at room temperature
* An INSTEC temperature stage with BNC feedthroughs for electrical connections, for operando measurements on devices
* Transient absorption down to 77 K with micrometric spatial resolution is being developed

## Nonlinear spectroscopy

* Four-wave mixing and wavelength-resolved femtosecond self-diffraction
* Second-harmonic, third-harmonic and sum-frequency generation
* Chiroptical spectroscopies with circularly polarized light

## Raman microscopy

* A HORIBA confocal Raman microscope, operating at room temperature with excitation at 532 nm and 633 nm

## Detection and diagnostics

* Visible and infrared spectrometers; the visible spectrometer is equipped with a camera acquiring at 100 kHz
* CCD cameras for imaging, including in the infrared
* An autocorrelator and an energy meter for pulse characterization

## Access and collaboration

We welcome enquiries about measurements on external samples. A first contact is an initial discussion of feasibility, not an automatic commitment to measurements. Access to the laboratory is collaborative and subject to training and laser-safety procedures. See [Collaborate with us](/lab/#collaborate-with-us) for what to include in a first email.
