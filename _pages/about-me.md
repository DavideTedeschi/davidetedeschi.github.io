I am a Tenure-track Assistant Professor in Experimental Condensed Matter Physics in the Department of Physical and Chemical Sciences at the University of L'Aquila. Together with Carino Ferrante (CNR-SPIN), I co-lead the [Nonlinear Ultrafast Spectroscopy Laboratory](/lab/), in close collaboration with Andrea Marini on the theory of nonlinear and chiral light–matter interactions. In the lab we use ultrafast and nonlinear optics to study how light interacts with matter, from two-dimensional semiconductors and epsilon-near-zero materials to chiral molecules and drugs. My background is in the optical spectroscopy of semiconductor nanostructures (quantum dots, nanowires and 2D materials), which I studied at Sapienza University of Rome before spending four years in industry and applied research at Leonardo and ENEA.

## My background

I received the B.S. (2012) and M.S. (2014) degrees in Physics, both cum laude, and the Ph.D. in Mathematical Models for Engineering, Electromagnetism and Nanosciences (2017) from Sapienza University of Rome. During my Ph.D., supervised by Antonio Polimeni, I studied the optoelectronic properties of InP nanowires and the effect of proton irradiation on transition-metal dichalcogenides, which led to the discovery of single-layer domes forming on multilayer flakes. My magneto-optical measurements at the High Field Magnet Laboratory in Nijmegen were awarded the "Paolo Mazzoldi" Prize of the Italian Physical Society in 2016.

In 2018 I joined, as a postdoctoral researcher, the newly established Nanophotonics group led by Rinaldo Trotta at Sapienza, funded by an ERC grant. I contributed to setting up the laboratory and to the group's first entanglement-swapping experiment, completed within its first year, using photons emitted on demand by semiconductor quantum dots; I also worked on quantum teleportation with the same sources. I then extended this work to 2D materials, using piezoelectric devices to tune the energy and brightness of single-photon emitters in WSe<sub>2</sub> monolayers.

I then spent four years in industry and applied research, gaining first-hand experience of how research is translated into technologies and services. At Leonardo I worked on the integration and validation of the power laser head of ATLID, the UV lidar of the European EarthCARE satellite mission, designed to study clouds, aerosols and their role in Earth's radiation balance. In 2021 I took a permanent research position at ENEA, where I worked on femtosecond stimulated Raman spectroscopy of gases and 2D materials and on the optical characterization of materials for batteries, catalysis and renewable energy, including a secondment at Monolithos in Athens within a European RISE project. In the same years I completed an Executive Master in Advanced Management: Space Economy at Luiss Business School. I joined the University of L'Aquila in November 2024.

I have co-authored more than 20 peer-reviewed journal articles, including papers in *Advanced Materials*, *ACS Nano*, *Nano Letters* and *Physical Review Letters*, and one book chapter.

## Projects and service

Our current projects include the QuantERA III project SiNPhoni (On-chip Moiré Single-Photon Emitters for Quantum Communications), an international consortium coordinated by the University of Oviedo in which our group is the L'Aquila partner unit. Other recent projects that have supported the development of the laboratory include the European Innovation Council (EIC) Pathfinder Open project Twisted Nano, the VITALITY innovation ecosystem and the siRNA-SPR nanomedicine project.

I co-organized ICOP 2026, the Italian Conference on Optics and Photonics, together with its co-located workshop PhotoChi, and the EULiST Blended Intensive Programme "Principles of Quantum and Nonlinear Photonics", and I served as Topic Chair at the IEEE Photonics Conference in 2025 and 2026.
