My research focuses on the optical properties and ultrafast dynamics of condensed-matter systems. I combine nonlinear optics, femtosecond spectroscopy and Raman microscopy to study semiconductor nanostructures, two-dimensional and epsilon-near-zero materials, chiral systems and biological samples.

The experiments are carried out in the [Nonlinear Ultrafast Spectroscopy Laboratory](/lab/), which I co-lead with Carino Ferrante (CNR-SPIN), in close collaboration with Andrea Marini on the theory of nonlinear and chiral light–matter interactions.

## Research lines

### Nonlinear optics

*How can the nonlinear optical response of a material be measured directly across its spectrum, and how do free carriers shape harmonic generation in epsilon-near-zero materials?*

We use femtosecond laser pulses for four-wave-mixing and harmonic-generation experiments. Using wavelength-resolved femtosecond self-diffraction, a four-wave-mixing technique, we showed that the self-diffracted signal directly measures the spectrum of the third-order nonlinear susceptibility. Our current work focuses on second-harmonic, third-harmonic and sum-frequency generation in epsilon-near-zero (ENZ) conductive oxide films, and on the role of electron depletion in these processes.

Selected work: L. Assogna, …, A. Marini, **D. Tedeschi**, C. Ferrante, "Femtosecond self-diffraction as a measure of the nonlinear response spectrum", *Optics Letters* 51, 1832 (2026). [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1364/OL.582839)

### Transient absorption spectroscopy

*How do excited states evolve, and how do excitons move, in organic solar cells and 2D heterostructures?*

We use femtosecond transient absorption to follow how excited states evolve in time. In organic solar cells, we track the excited-state dynamics that govern how absorbed light is turned into charges. For two-dimensional semiconductors, we are developing transient absorption measurements at 77 K with micrometric spatial resolution, to resolve exciton diffusion in space and time in van der Waals heterostructures of transition-metal dichalcogenides. This work is part of the QuantERA project SiNPhoni on on-chip moiré single-photon emitters for quantum communications.

### Chiral photonics for drug discovery and safety

*Can the enantiomeric imbalance of a chiral drug solution be measured in nanolitre volumes?*

Many drugs are chiral, and their two mirror-image forms can act very differently in the body. Today, enantiomeric discrimination relies mainly on chiral high-performance liquid chromatography (HPLC), a bulky laboratory technique. Building on the European Innovation Council (EIC) Pathfinder Open project Twisted Nano, we are working to demonstrate a compact nanophotonic device capable of measuring the enantiomeric imbalance of chiral solutions in nanolitre volumes. The experiments are closely integrated with theoretical modelling of chiral light–matter interactions developed with Andrea Marini.

Selected work:

* A. Alessandrini, …, **D. Tedeschi**, A. Marini, "High-performance chiral mirrors by twisted anisotropic photonic crystals", *Physical Review Applied* 23, 064027 (2025). [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevapplied.23.064027)
* A. Vetrano, …, C. Ferrante, **D. Tedeschi**, …, C. Iacobucci, "Enantiopurity-dependent peptide coacervates and asymmetric organocatalysis", *Small*, e14872 (2026). [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1002/smll.202514872)

### Raman spectroscopy

*How much nanoplastic accumulates inside cells, and how does the Raman response of solids change with strain and polarization?*

We use Raman microscopy to quantify the accumulation of nanoplastics in biological samples, both in vitro and in vivo. We also perform Raman spectroscopy on solid-state samples, studying how the Raman response changes with the polarization of light and with strain.

Selected work: F. Perrella, …, C. Ferrante, **D. Tedeschi**, "Quantifying cellular nanoplastics accumulation using Raman microscopy", *ACS Omega* 11, 66784–66794 (2026). [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1021/acsomega.6c06443)
