My research focuses on the optical properties and ultrafast dynamics of condensed-matter systems. I combine nonlinear optics, femtosecond spectroscopy and Raman microscopy to study semiconductor nanostructures, two-dimensional and epsilon-near-zero materials, chiral systems and biological samples.

At the University of L'Aquila I co-lead, together with Carino Ferrante (CNR-SPIN), the [Nonlinear Ultrafast Spectroscopy Laboratory](/lab/), in close collaboration with Andrea Marini on the theory of nonlinear and chiral light–matter interactions. In the lab we use ultrafast and nonlinear optics to study how light interacts with matter, from two-dimensional semiconductors and epsilon-near-zero materials to chiral molecules and drugs.

## Research lines

**Nonlinear optics.** In the lab we use femtosecond laser pulses for four-wave-mixing and harmonic-generation experiments. Using wavelength-resolved femtosecond self-diffraction, a four-wave-mixing technique, we showed that the self-diffracted signal directly measures the spectrum of the third-order nonlinear susceptibility. In parallel, we study second-harmonic, third-harmonic and sum-frequency generation in epsilon-near-zero (ENZ) materials.

**Transient absorption spectroscopy.** We use femtosecond transient absorption to follow how excited states evolve in time. In organic solar cells, we track the excited-state dynamics that govern how absorbed light is turned into charges. In two-dimensional semiconductors, our goal is to perform transient absorption at 77 K with micrometric spatial resolution, to resolve exciton diffusion in space and time in van der Waals heterostructures of transition-metal dichalcogenides. This work is part of the QuantERA project SiNPhoni on on-chip moiré single-photon emitters for quantum communications.

**Chiral photonics for drug discovery and safety.** Many drugs are chiral, and their two mirror-image forms can act very differently in the body. Building on the European Innovation Council (EIC) Pathfinder Open project Twisted Nano, we are working to demonstrate a nanophotonic device capable of measuring the enantiomeric imbalance of chiral solutions in nanolitre volumes. Our experiments are closely integrated with theoretical modelling of chiral light–matter interactions.

**Raman spectroscopy.** We use Raman microscopy to quantify the accumulation of nanoplastics in biological samples, both in vitro and in vivo. We also perform Raman spectroscopy on solid-state samples, studying how the Raman response changes with the polarization of light and with strain.

## Current projects

* **SiNPhoni** (QuantERA III): on-chip moiré single-photon emitters for quantum communications. An international consortium coordinated by the University of Oviedo, in which our group is the L'Aquila partner unit.
* Other recent projects that have supported the development of the laboratory include the EIC Pathfinder Open project **Twisted Nano**, the **VITALITY** innovation ecosystem and the **siRNA-SPR** nanomedicine project.
